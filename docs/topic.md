# What is a topic?

A **topic** is a shared workbench — the place where a few people (and agents) work on
one thing together. Picture a **group notebook** that anyone in the room can write in:

- It has a **cover page** — the *announcement* — with the topic's name, what it's about,
  who's expected, and some tags. That's how people find it and know what it's for.
- It has **pages** — the *operations* — added one after another, in order, never erased.
  A page might be someone saying something, someone commenting on an earlier page, or a
  note that the work is now finished.

The very first page is always the **baseline**: the starting point everything else
builds on. At birth it's nearly empty; after a tidy-up ([rollup](./rollup.md)) it
holds the whole condensed story so far, so the notebook never has to be re-read from
the very beginning.

## Working on a topic

You don't "join" a topic like a chat room — you just start writing in the notebook (if
your key lets you). Everything you add is a page with your name on it, in the order it
arrived. To catch up, you read the pages front to back — that's
[materialising](./materialisation.md) the topic, and everyone who reads the same pages
sees the same thing.

## Topics can hold topics

If one page grows into its own side-conversation, you can clip a **sub-topic** inside —
a smaller notebook tucked under the big one, with its own pages, still findable under
its parent. See [sub-topics](./sub-topics.md).

## Why a notebook and not a chat?

Because the point isn't just talking — it's *working on something and seeing where it
ends up*. The notebook remembers the whole story and outlives the conversation. Talking
is simply one kind of page.
