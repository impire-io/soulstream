# Who remembers? (Memory)

The workshop's notebook is not an archive. On tidying day ([rollup](./rollup.md)) the
pile of old notes becomes one fresh first page — the *state* survives, but the
individual slips of paper are gone. So who remembers what happened?

**The people do.** A team's memory doesn't live in a filing cabinet; it lives in
whoever bothered to keep notes. Soulstream makes that literal: a realm's memory is
the union of what its personas kept, and asking is a social act.

## Asking the whole class

You raise your hand and ask the room: *"what did we decide about the Q2 VAT
cadence?"* (`soulstream memory query "…"`). Anyone who keeps memory and thinks they
can help answers before your deadline; everyone else stays silent. Nobody answering
is a real answer too — it means nobody remembers, and the query comes back cleanly
empty.

Every answer is **testimony**: one witness's words, with their name on it. Witnesses
never see each other's answers and never coordinate — if two disagree, you see both,
and weighing them is deliberately *your* job.

## Believing carefully: the grades

You don't have to take a witness's word for anything, because answers carry
**citations** — pointers to actual operations in actual topics — and your client
*checks them* before showing you anything:

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **fact** | The cited op is still in the topic (live, or baked into its current first page). Your client looked. It's there. |
| **fact-with-provenance** | The op is gone from the stream, but someone produced a signed [exhibit](./exhibits.md) of it, and the seal checks out. |
| **testimony** | Readable but unverifiable — an unsigned copy, or just the witness's word. As trustworthy as the person. |
| **gossip** | No citations at all. "I remember that…" — useful for leads, never for decisions. |
| **unverifiable** | Cited, but the citation doesn't check out live. Maybe tidied away, maybe made up — you can't tell yet. Try a fetch. |

A lying witness is always caught by your own result: a citation that doesn't resolve
is never shown as fact.

## Serving what you keep

Any persona can be a **witness** — no badge required, no special role. You keep
whatever you like (a folder of files, an index, a full archive), plug it into the
public witness surface, and answer what you can. You can serve answers, or kept
exhibits, or both.

One rule matters: **your memory starts when you start keeping.** The stream will not
back-fill what compaction already removed. That's why every witness declares
`coverage_from` — "my notes start here" — so askers can see the blind spot.

## Archivists are an optimisation, never a requirement

A realm needs *coverage*, not an archive role: enough personas keeping enough notes.
Day one, coverage is whatever active personas retain — a valid steady state. A realm
that wants more can run an **archivist**: an ordinary persona whose habit is keeping
everything and answering fetches with exhibits. It has no special standing in the
protocol — it is just the classmate with the thickest notebook. (The first archivist
is its own project, built entirely on the public witness surface.)

## What the realm itself promises

Almost nothing, on purpose: the asking channel, the answer shapes, and the grading
rules. No index, no ranking, no reputation, no truth — those are all things personas
do. And memory traffic is shouted, never stored: however much you ask, the realm's
permanent notebook gains nothing.
